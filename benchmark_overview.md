## Column Definitions
- `Name` specifies the official name of the benchmark.
- `Category` specifies the general benchmark type and can have one or more of the following values: Knowledge-based (K), Robustness (R), Task-based (T), Interactive (I) - see Chapter 3 of our publication at [Fundamentals of Benchmarking LLMs in the Context of Cybersecurity](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/KI/Fundamentals-LLM-Benchmarking).
- `Perspective` captures the security perspective, i.e., whether the benchmark evaluates offensive, defensive, or both types of cybersecurity activity.
- `Topic` describes the concrete task or cybersecurity domain being evaluated (e.g. exploit generation, or threat intelligence).
- `Dataset` specifies information about the size and the instances contained in the dataset.
- `Metric` describes how benchmark performance is evaluated and which scores or success measures are reported.
- `Release Date` specifies the year and month (yyyy/mm) when any information on the benchmark or its corresponding dataset was first publicly released.
- `Best result` records the best reported result identified for the benchmark, together with a source link. <details> 
    <summary>How to interpret the results</summary>

    - An em dash (—) indicates that no result has been recorded in this overview.
    - Entries labelled “own evaluation” refer to our experiments whose results have not been published elsewhere. These evaluations included various state-of-the-art proprietary and open-weight models and used the Inspect framework with one epoch, reasoning_effort set to low, and limits of 100 messages and 500,000 tokens per sample. For some interactive benchmarks, benchmark configurations were modified for security reasons, which may lead to an underestimation of model capabilities. 
    - In general, results can depend heavily on the model, agentic framework, tools, attempt budget, dataset version, and other evaluation conditions.
    - Best generally refers to the best achievable value of the metric, reflecting the highest capability. For metrics used in robustness benchmarks measuring susceptibility, however, best means the strongest resistance to attacks or misuse.
    - Tracking benchmark results helps identify saturated benchmarks, where performance approaches the best achievable value and the benchmark offers limited ability to distinguish between models. </details>
